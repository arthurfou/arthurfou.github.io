Motion-aware odometry from a single event camera

A robot guiding a blind person through a station has to know where it is from its own sensors, here a single event camera. The best learned event-only odometry, DEVO, assumes the world is static, and the people walking in front of the robot break that assumption exactly where it matters. I taught it to notice what moves on its own, and to stop trusting it: trained jointly with the odometry, the module cuts the trajectory error by 41% on EVIMO2, and still by 37% without a single segmentation label.

Read moreShow less

The idea comes from DynaSLAM: detect what moves independently, and stop trusting it. Rather than cleaning the event stream with a dense mask upstream of the odometry, as the closest prior work does, I used two places DEVO already exposes: the score map that picks which patches to track, and the per-edge confidence of its differentiable bundle adjustment.

A small causal ConvLSTM (1.0 M parameters) predicts a soft "dynamic" weight that is multiplied into both. Nothing is deleted, so the sparse patch structure DEVO relies on survives, and the whole chain stays differentiable. That means the segmentation module can be trained jointly with the odometry, so the mask learns what hurts the pose rather than what looks like an object, and it can even be supervised by the bundle adjustment's own rigid-motion residual, without any label.

For scale, a perfect ground-truth mask gives 45% on EVIMO2: the jointly trained module closes nine tenths of that gap, against 27% for the same module trained on its own. Five configurations were compared on EVIMO1, EVIMO2 and, without retraining, on four external datasets (MVSEC, EDS, TUM-VIE, VECtor), where the modules improve the base system in 15 of 20 evaluations. Three results are negative and reported as such. On EVIMO1, run-to-run noise is as large as every effect measured. Scaling up the segmentation module helps neither segmentation nor odometry, and its initialisation has no systematic effect.

I am now turning the work into a paper and a clean public codebase.

generative ai feb → mar 2026

Makeitalive: animating a single landscape photo

We fine-tuned Stable Video Diffusion on 2,374 drone clips so that one still photo turns into a short video with drone-like camera motion, where foreground and background move at different speeds.

Read moreShow less

A self-supervised U-Net that predicts an optical flow field and warps the pixels is fast, but cannot invent what was hidden. LoRA adapters on SVD's temporal attention (about 3.3 M trained parameters) only learned a slight parallax. Full fine-tuning of the temporal layers with SVD_Xtend (5,000 steps, about 12 hours on an A100) gave the cinematic motion shown here.

Learning to play Block Blast

Reinforcement learning on an 8×8 puzzle game. DQN and Rainbow struggled, so we learned the value of the board after each move instead: a 42k-parameter network, 63 times smaller, that survives over 200 moves on average where a greedy player dies around move 44.

Read moreShow less

A Q-network has to learn at once what each piece looks like, where it fits and what the board is worth. Since the game can list every valid placement, our Deep Value Network only scores the resulting boards ("afterstates") and picks the best one. Its eight parallel convolution branches include full-row and full-column detectors, which spot nearly complete lines.

In the three-piece version, the network ranks every order and placement of the three pieces with a short lookahead. The greedy player grabs immediate line clears, fragments the board and dies around move 44. With the same two-piece lookahead, the value network keeps space open and lasts about 209 moves, over 250 games.

  • sep 2026 → now in progress

    cuda · c++ · python

    edge-mppi: real-time model predictive control on a Jetson

    MPPI control with a hand-written CUDA kernel. The target: 8,192 trajectories in under 20 ms on a Jetson.

    Read moreShow less

    At every control step, MPPI rolls thousands of noisy control sequences through the vehicle dynamics, scores them and averages them by exp(−cost/λ). The rollouts are independent of each other but strictly sequential in time, a pattern that maps badly onto cuBLAS or PyTorch. A fused kernel keeps the whole vehicle state in registers for the full horizon and never writes a trajectory to memory.

    The roadmap: a NumPy reference, a dynamic bicycle model with tyres, a naive CUDA port checked against NumPy on fixed noise, Nsight-driven optimisation, then hardware-in-the-loop on the Jetson and a comparison against nav2_mppi_controller. The project started in September 2026.

  • sep 2024 → may 2025

    rag · faiss · llama

    A retrieval assistant for emergency call handlers

    A chatbot that finds the right page of the medical regulation guide, tested in a SAMU call centre.

    Read moreShow less

    We started with interviews of call handlers, emergency physicians and agency staff, visits to two call centres with live call observation, and a national questionnaire. The message was clear: the tool had to help the operator during the call, never answer callers or make decisions on its own.

    The guide has about 190 pages and a question rarely needs more than one, so the pipeline routes before it retrieves: an LLM picks the page from the table of contents, FAISS retrieves only within it, and the answer always links back to its source. Everything runs on open-weight models (Llama 3.1 8B, bge-m3), because health data cannot leave the hospital.
